Dr. Esther Lehman Kawira, MD, FAAFP

Physician • Medical Director • Researcher • Rural Health Leader

Biography

Dr. Esther Lehman Kawira has devoted more than four decades to medicine, medical education, research and community health in northern Tanzania.

Born and raised in Indiana in the United States, she earned her medical degree from the Indiana University School of Medicine in 1978, completed her residency in family medicine, and became a Diplomate of the American Board of Family Medicine. She later completed postdoctoral training in medical genetics and became a Fellow of the American Academy of Family Physicians.

From 1981 to 1983, Dr. Kawira served as an instructor in medical genetics in the Department of Biology at the University of Notre Dame. This period of academic teaching and genetics training helped establish the combination of clinical medicine, research and education that would characterize her later career in Tanzania.

Shirati, 1983

A Life of Service in Shirati

In 1980, Dr. Kawira married Josiah Okeyo Kawira, a native of Shirati, Tanzania. In 1983, the couple moved to Shirati, where they established their home, raised their family and devoted their professional lives to serving the surrounding communities. Dr. Kawira initially worked through Shirati Hospital and Eastern Mennonite Missions, providing clinical care while developing a particular interest in the health challenges facing children and families in rural northern Tanzania.

Over the following decades, Dr. Kawira became one of the region’s most experienced physicians and an important bridge between rural Tanzanian health care and international medical education. She treated patients, supervised health workers, coordinated visiting medical students and professionals, and helped create opportunities for physicians, nurses, physician assistants, students and researchers from abroad to learn alongside Tanzanian colleagues and serve rural communities. Her work emphasized that international partnerships are strongest when they build local capacity and grow from long-term relationships rather than short-term visits.

In 2005, Dr. Kawira joined other local leaders in establishing the Shirati Health, Education and Development Foundation. When SHED formally began operations in 2006, she became its Medical Director and helped establish Sota Health Clinic as one of the organization’s first major programs. The clinic initially operated from rented premises before moving into a permanent SHED facility near Magamaga in 2013. Through Sota and SHED’s wider health programs, Dr. Kawira helped provide preventive, diagnostic and curative care while building relationships with hospitals, universities, nonprofit organizations and individual supporters.

Research

Burkitt Lymphoma and the EMBLEM Study

A particularly important focus of Dr. Kawira’s career has been Burkitt lymphoma, a childhood cancer that occurs with unusually high frequency in equatorial Africa. Her years of clinical experience caring for children with Burkitt lymphoma contributed to a wider body of research examining the disease in East Africa. She became the Tanzania Principal Investigator for the Epidemiology of Burkitt Lymphoma in East African Children and Minors (EMBLEM) Study, a multinational research initiative supported by the U.S. National Cancer Institute and National Institutes of Health. Working with researchers and clinicians in Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda and the United States, Dr. Kawira helped connect rural patient care with internationally significant epidemiological research.

SHED served as an important local platform for the Tanzania component of EMBLEM, supporting research administration, field activities, case identification and collaboration with institutions including Bugando Medical Centre. The project demonstrated that a community-based Tanzanian organization could successfully participate in complex multinational medical research while maintaining close relationships with the communities from which that research emerged.

Dr. Kawira’s leadership extended beyond cancer care and research. Through SHED she helped develop rural medical outreach, maternal and child health services, professional education and collaborations with institutions in Tanzania and abroad. She supported the growth of Roche Health Center, helped develop partnerships with visiting universities and medical teams, and encouraged SHED’s expansion into areas including sickle-cell screening and care.

Her work has also contributed to the scientific literature. Dr. Kawira has authored and co-authored publications in medical genetics, family medicine, tropical medicine and pediatric oncology, including research examining the epidemiology and incidence of Burkitt lymphoma in northern Tanzania. Her career has consistently connected clinical medicine with the larger questions of why diseases affect particular communities and how research can improve the care available to those communities.

For Dr. Kawira, medicine has never been separate from community development. Her work through SHED reflects the belief that better health requires more than treatment alone. Clean water, nutrition, education, trained health professionals, accessible facilities and strong local institutions all shape whether families can live healthy and productive lives.

From her arrival in Shirati in 1983 through the growth of SHED Foundation, Dr. Kawira’s career has been defined by an unusually sustained commitment to one community. Patients she first encountered as a young physician became parents and grandparents; visiting students became physicians and long-term collaborators; and projects that began as ideas developed into health facilities, research partnerships and programs that continued for years.

Her legacy within SHED is therefore larger than any individual clinic or research project. She helped establish a model in which local service, scientific research and international partnership reinforce one another, a model that continues to shape SHED Foundation as it enters its third decade.
